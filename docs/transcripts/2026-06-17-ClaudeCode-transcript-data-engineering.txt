❯ what is a security master / identifier mapping?                               

● A security master is an authoritative reference table that gives every
  security one canonical, permanent identity — and an identifier mapping (or
  "crosswalk") is the lookup that translates all the other codes people use for
  that security back to it.

  The reason you need one: a ticker is not a stable, unique key, even though
  everyone treats it like one. Concretely, the messiness we already have in this
  repo:

  - Tickers vary by exchange and vendor. Our monthly_returns.csv columns are
  Yahoo-style — NOKIA.HE (Helsinki), RIO.L (London), 7203.T (Toyota, Tokyo),
  MC.PA (Paris), NESN.SW (Swiss). The same companies in another database carry
  totally different symbols. That mismatch is exactly why our join of holdings
  against msci_world_equities.csv matched only 8–25 of 130.
  - The same company appears under multiple symbols. We literally hold both NOK
  (Nokia's US ADR) and NOKIA.HE (Nokia in Helsinki). Without a master you can't
  tell they're the same issuer — you risk double-counting or mapping
  fundamentals to the wrong line.
  - Tickers get renamed and recycled. Facebook FB → META; when a company dies,
  its old ticker can be reassigned to a different company later. So "ticker X in
  2019" ≠ "ticker X today."
  - Delisted names lose their symbol entirely. Wirecard (WDI.DE) stops resolving
  after its collapse — but a security master keeps the entity alive with a
  permanent ID so you can still pull its history.

  What fixes it is a set of stable identifiers that are designed to be unique
  and persistent:

  - Cross-vendor standards: ISIN, SEDOL, CUSIP (codes attached to the security
  itself, not the trading symbol).
  - Vendor permanent IDs: PERMNO (CRSP), GVKEY (Compustat), FIGI (Bloomberg),
  PermID/RIC (Refinitiv) — these survive delisting and renaming.

  The security master is the table that holds, per security: a permanent ID, its
  name (with history), all its tickers/ISINs/SEDOLs, listing exchange,
  currency, country, and the dates each mapping was valid. The crosswalk then
  lets you do joins like:

  (ticker "NOKIA.HE", date 2019-03)  →  ISIN FI0009000681  →  GVKEY 015787  →
  our_id 42

  so the holding, its returns, its fundamentals, and its sector all line up on
  the same entity — instead of silently dropping 80% of the names like a raw
  ticker join does.

  Analogy: it's the "single source of truth" record in a CRM (one canonical
  customer, many aliases), or a primary key plus an alias lookup table in a
  database. For an international portfolio like this one — multiple exchanges,
  ADRs, currencies, and delistings — it's the piece that makes everything else
  joinable, which is why it shows up as a distinct need from fundamentals.

❯ Before we move on, should we use a weighed harmonic mean instead of a naive

  weighed harmonic mean, and median) for the ratios in the aggregated table?    

● Great question — and your instinct is right, with an important caveat. Let me
  lay out the finance, then implement the sound version.

  For price multiples (P/E, P/B, P/S, P/FCF, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales), the 
  value-weighted harmonic mean is the correct aggregation. The portfolio's true
  P/E is Σ price / Σ earnings, and algebraically that equals the value-weighted
  harmonic mean of the constituents' P/Es — not the arithmetic mean. The
  arithmetic mean systematically overstates multiples (Jensen's inequality) and
  gets dominated by low-earnings names. This is exactly why our naive P/E came
  out ~45–55 while the median was ~31. Morningstar/MSCI aggregate portfolio
  multiples this way.

  But harmonic is wrong for the others. The "percentages" — yields
  (earnings/FCF/dividend), margins, ROE/ROIC, growth rates, leverage, z/f-scores
  — are not price-per-fundamental ratios. For those the value-weighted
  arithmetic mean is correct (e.g., portfolio dividend yield = Σdiv/ΣMV =
  value-weighted mean of yields). Harmonic on them is meaningless and blows up
  on the negative/zero values several of them have (negative growth, negative
  ROCE).

  So the clean version of "provide all three":
  - All 25 features: weighted mean + median.
  - The 6 price multiples only: also weighted harmonic mean (over positive
  values, so negative-earnings names don't poison it — standard index practice).
